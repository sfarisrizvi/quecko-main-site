## 2. THE CHALLENGE

### Headline
KYC and AML obligations require collecting exactly the kind of sensitive personal data that privacy regulators scrutinize most closely — identity documents, biometric data, transaction history. Any platform handling this data across borders is almost certainly a data controller under multiple overlapping privacy regimes simultaneously, and Travel Rule data-sharing between VASPs adds yet another cross-border transfer to manage.

### Body
Privacy compliance and AML compliance can pull in opposite directions — AML wants more data collected and shared between institutions, while privacy law wants data minimized and tightly controlled. Getting the balance wrong risks fines that dwarf development costs on either side.

## 12. FAQs

**Does this apply if we only serve one country?**
Often yes — cloud infrastructure, third-party processors, and international users frequently trigger cross-border obligations even for "local" platforms.

**Can this integrate with our KYC/AML build?**
Yes, data privacy strategy is designed to work alongside KYC/AML data collection requirements, not against them.

**How does Travel Rule data-sharing affect our privacy obligations?**
Sharing originator/beneficiary data with a foreign VASP counterparty is itself a cross-border data transfer, requiring its own legal basis and safeguards under regimes like GDPR.

**What happens if we're already operating without a formal privacy review?**
We start with a data flow audit to establish current exposure, then prioritize remediation based on the highest-risk gaps first.
