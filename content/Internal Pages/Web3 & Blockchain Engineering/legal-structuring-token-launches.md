## 2. THE CHALLENGE

### Headline
Regulators have sharpened their classification frameworks significantly — the 2026 SEC/CFTC joint interpretation now splits digital assets into five categories (digital commodities, digital collectibles, digital tools, stablecoins, and digital securities), and FATF has been explicit that a token labeled "utility" is still a virtual asset if it's actually used for payment or investment. Misclassification risk increases sharply the moment a token includes profit-sharing, staking rewards, or roadmap-linked value promises.

### Body
Getting token classification wrong is one of the most expensive mistakes a Web3 project can make — retroactive enforcement, exchange delisting, or investor lawsuits. And classification isn't static: a token's legal character can shift after launch if governance, staking mechanics, or profit-sharing features are added later.

## 12. FAQs

**Can you determine if our token is a security?**
We provide classification analysis and coordinate with securities counsel for formal legal opinions where required.

**Do you help structure DAOs or foundations?**
Yes, foundation and DAO legal wrapper design is part of this service.

**Can our token's classification change after launch?**
Yes — adding staking rewards, profit-sharing, or centralizing governance control can shift a token's legal character even post-launch, which is why ongoing monitoring matters.

**What's the difference between a digital commodity and a digital security under the new framework?**
Broadly, digital commodities are treated like tradable goods under CFTC oversight, while digital securities carry investment-contract characteristics under SEC oversight — the specific facts of your token's design determine which applies.
